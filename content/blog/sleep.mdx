I've never been a great sleeper. I grew up in one of those houses built in the early nineties that had a room over the garage. It was huge, way bigger than it needed to be. The width of the room was the same , spanning all three car bays with two unfinished crawlspaces.
I could re-arrange the furniture whenver I wanted, in whatever orientation that the king (me) saw fit. At night, though, it scared the absolute fuck out of me.

Only in the last few years have I realized how much I truly remember from that bedroom. When it was hot out, I would sleep with the window open and when it was cold it was nearly unbearable. I can still feel the carpet on my feet.
The worst, and most uncomfortable part, was nights. My family operated (and still does) on a strict bedtime schedule, so growing up we went to bed early no matter what. There could have been an F4 tornado
pulling 120-year old oak trees out of the ground across the street and I would have been in bed between 8:00 and 9:00 PM.

Maybe it's that I can barely survive without my contacts in, but I'm not a huge fan of the dark. That bedroom curated my fear of the dark.
It talked to me at night, like fucking Bane. I woke to every sound, assuming that something was going to jump out of a dark corner. What would it do? I don't know.
But I took to protecting myself with the covers like any child would. If that wasn't enough, my maternal grandmother lived out in the middle
of the woods next to the fucking interstate. The house was in a clearing, so if somebody was intent on murdering you in the night you'd see it
coming (and hear nothing but the interstate traffic).

As I got to high school, a bedtime turned to a curfew. Curfew was generally 10 or 11 PM, and I don't know if I actually recall any issues sleeping or falling asleep. I had grown about 8 inches and thinned out,
and I was enjoying the popularity that came with no longer being a little fat curly headed fuck. I was just _busy_. I'd wake up at 5:00 AM to get to the
gym before school (it was literally across the street) to lift with the lacrosse team before class. After class, we had practice. After practice, homework. Then bed. Felt like a few years of the same thing.  
I guess I just got my shit done during the day and went to bed at a reasonable time.

I don't think I truly identified the feeling of being _tired_ until I got to college. In the earlier years (I was there an unreasonable amount of time), I chalked
that up to staying out until 3:45 in the morning and having to get up and operate like a normal person the next day. I probably should have
taken a gap year, but I decided to go to a school where I didn't know anybody and see if I could drink 100,000 beers in a semester. It's possible that I got close.

"I'll sleep when I'm dead" was something I think that an early twenties version of Jake truly believed, and he was stupid as shit. The "fuck around" line eventually intercepted the "find out"
line on my life's decision-making chart. I have a distinct memory of peeking out of the blinds in Athens, Georgia, in the middle of the night thinking somebody was watching me.
I had been awake over 40 hours for the second time in a week.

It used to bother me that mentioning sleep problems to anyone would beget advice you never asked for, but now I just smile and thank the person. No, in all my time having issues sleeping,
I have never heard of taking magnesium. Some days it feels like I am being tortured by my own brain,

Even after the worst night's sleep, I wouldn't trade my life and how it's turned out for anything in the world.
